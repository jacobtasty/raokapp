Advent of Kindness: 25 acts of kindness for December

By the RAOK team · 18 November 2026 · 6-minute read

An Advent calendar where, instead of opening a door, you do one small kind thing. Twenty-five days, twenty-five acts, almost all of them free. Here's the full list, ready to print or follow along.

What is an Advent of Kindness?

It's a simple idea: from 1 to 25 December, do one small act of kindness each day. Some are for people you love, some for neighbours or strangers, and a few are for the planet. None of them need much time, and most cost nothing.

December is a lovely time to try it. People are busier, more tired and sometimes lonelier than usual, so a small kindness goes a long way.

The 25 acts

  1. 1

    Day 1: Send a “thinking of you” message

    Pick someone you haven't spoken to for a while. A text, a card or a voice note: “I was thinking of you today. How are you?”

  2. 2

    Day 2: Thank your postie or a delivery driver

    December is their busiest month. Say thanks at the door, or leave a note for them to find.

  3. 3

    Day 3: Add to the food bank box

    Drop a few tins, some pasta or toiletries in the collection box at your supermarket. Food banks are especially busy in winter.

  4. 4

    Day 4: Let someone go ahead of you

    Christmas queues are long. If someone behind you has one or two things, wave them through.

  5. 5

    Day 5: Send a card to a neighbour

    Pick a neighbour you don't know well and pop a Christmas card through their door, signed with your house number.

  6. 6

    Day 6: Thank someone by name

    Shop staff work long hours in December. Read the name badge and say “Thank you, Sam, you've been really helpful.”

  7. 7

    Day 7: Pass on something warm

    Donate a coat, hat, scarf or gloves you no longer wear to a charity shop or a local coat appeal.

  8. 8

    Day 8: Check in on someone who lives alone

    Knock on an older neighbour's door or call a relative. Offer something specific: “I'm going to the shop, do you need anything?”

  9. 9

    Day 9: Leave a kind review

    Give a small local business you love a five-star review, and mention one person or thing by name.

  10. 10

    Day 10: Make someone a hot drink

    Without being asked. At home, at work or for a friend who's had a long week.

  11. 11

    Day 11: Tell a colleague they did well

    Name one thing they did this year that made a difference, and say it in front of others if you can.

  12. 12

    Day 12: Do a five-minute litter pick

    Grab a bag and some gloves and tidy your street or the path to the shops.

  13. 13

    Day 13: Share a skill

    Help someone set up a new phone, follow a recipe or wrap an awkward present.

  14. 14

    Day 14: Look after the birds

    Put out seeds, fat balls or a shallow dish of water. Wild birds struggle when the ground is frozen.

  15. 15

    Day 15: Send a voice note to an older relative

    A minute of your voice means more than a text. Tell them something you remember about them.

  16. 16

    Day 16: Thank your bin collectors

    Leave a thank-you note or a small treat on your bin lid. It's an old tradition worth keeping.

  17. 17

    Day 17: Give to a toy or gift appeal

    Many supermarkets, churches and community groups collect presents for children and families who'd go without.

  18. 18

    Day 18: Help a busy parent

    Offer to wrap presents, pick something up or have the kids for an hour.

  19. 19

    Day 19: Thank someone who's working over Christmas

    Nurses, carers, care home staff, police, bus drivers: send a message or drop off something to say thank you.

  20. 20

    Day 20: Give one conversation your full attention

    Phone away, eyes up. Ask a follow-up question and really listen to the answer.

  21. 21

    Day 21: Reach out on the shortest day

    21 December is the darkest day of the year. Message someone who might be finding winter hard.

  22. 22

    Day 22: Help with heavy bags

    In the busy shops, offer to carry bags to a car or help with a pushchair on the stairs.

  23. 23

    Day 23: Invite someone who might be alone

    Ask a neighbour, colleague or friend who might be on their own to join you for a festive drink, a walk or a meal.

  24. 24

    Day 24: Share three things you're grateful for

    Write them down, then tell one person you're grateful for them, and why.

  25. 25

    Day 25: Tell the people around you what you love about them

    One sentence each, on Christmas Day. It might be the best present they get.

Tips for making it stick

  • Keep it small. The point is to do something every day, not something big once.
  • Swap days around. If an act doesn't suit you today, do another one and come back to it.
  • Do it with someone. A partner, friend, family or workplace doing it together is far more fun, and you'll remind each other.
  • Missed a day? That's fine. Be kind to yourself too. Just pick it up again tomorrow.
  • Keep people's privacy in mind. If you share what you did, leave out names and details that could identify anyone, especially children.

Why it's worth doing

Most of us underestimate how much our kindness means. In a series of experiments, psychologists Amit Kumar and Nicholas Epley asked people to do small acts of kindness, like giving a stranger in a park a hot chocolate. The people being kind consistently underestimated how good the other person felt (read about the study).

In other words, the small thing you nearly didn't bother with probably mattered more than you think.
